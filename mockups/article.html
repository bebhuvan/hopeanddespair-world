Question 01 · War & Peace

Is humanity becoming less violent?

The same evidence says yes and no. Drag the lens across time — the verdict is a function of how far back you stand.

Violent deaths per 100,000 · 10,000 years 8000 BCE — 2024
Verdict
YES.

Across the long sweep, the decline is real — states slowly tamed the raid, the feud, and the duel.

Vantage
The multi-disciplinary read
The historianconfidence · high

Hope  Compared with tribal and feudal pasts, the organized state has crowded out everyday private violence.

Despair  The same state perfected industrial-scale war when it chose to wage it.

The statisticianconfidence · medium

Hope  Per-capita battle deaths sit far below their historical band.

Despair  The denominator hides the dead; rare mega-wars dominate the true risk.

Someone living itconfidence · absolute

Despair  A global average is no comfort under a drone. My street is the only dataset that matters.

The forecasterconfidence · low

Despair  Cheap drones, autonomy, and disinformation lower the cost of mass harm.

Hope  Nuclear taboo and economic entanglement still raise the cost of total war.

The two strongest cases

Strongest case for hope

Interstate conquest has become rare and largely illegitimate. For most people alive today, the statistical chance of dying violently is the lowest it has ever been.

Strongest case for despair

That decline reversed after 2020. Battle deaths are climbing again, and the technologies that made killing expensive are becoming cheap. The trend is not a law.

What would change this verdict

A sustained five-year rise in global battle deaths would retire the long-run optimism. One nuclear exchange would end the argument entirely.
