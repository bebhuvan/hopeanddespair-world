Question 01 War & Peace

Is humanity becoming
less violent?

No single chart can answer it, so we don't ask one to. Five signals are read at once — and the verdict depends entirely on how far back you set the lens. Slide it across time and watch the evidence converge, then break apart.

◐ Before you read · caveats & blind spots
  • Figures in this draft are illustrative pending real ingestion — treat absolute levels as placeholders.
  • “Violence” here means direct lethal violence; it excludes coercion, structural harm, and the threat of force.
  • Pre-1900 numbers are regional and sparse — the global long arc is reconstructed, not measured.
  • The composite verdict is an editorial reading of the signals, not a computed index. Full method in §IV.

The long arcFIG. 6

Homicide has fallen for seven centuries.

a Pre-modern figures come from coroners’ rolls and court archives — reliable in their direction, rough in their absolute level.

Where parish and coroner records survive, the everyday violence of private life has collapsed. A medieval European was tens of times more likely to be murdered than a person living in the same cities today — a decline so long it predates the state's monopoly on force.

Homicides per 100,000 / year · Western Europe1300 — 2020
Source · Eisner (2003); Our World in Data — illustrative
Hope

A forty-fold fall. The medieval baseline of violent death has all but vanished from daily life.

Despair

“Rare” is not “none,” and the clean line is European — much of the world is far thinner on history.

Confusion

Definitions, coverage, and record-keeping all shift beneath the curve. The slope is real; the precision is not.

The recent breakFIG. 7

Displacement is at an all-time high.

The rate signals improved while the human totals did not. The number of people forced from their homes has roughly doubled in a decade, and the curve bends sharply upward after 2022 — a reminder that a falling death rate can sit beside a rising count of ruined lives.

Forcibly displaced people · millions, worldwide1951 — 2024
Source · UNHCR Global Trends — illustrative
Hope

More people are counted, sheltered, and resettled than any system in history could once manage.

Despair

120 million uprooted. The post-war institutions are buckling under a number they were never built for.

Confusion

“Displaced” bundles refugees, the internally displaced, and the stateless — very different fates under one line.

Who the average hidesFIG. 8

The global mean is a comfortable lie.

b A population-weighted mean would sit lower still — the unweighted average already buries the worst-off countries.

A single world line stays low and almost flat — and tells you nothing about where the violence actually lives. Pull the same measure apart by country and the average dissolves into catastrophe for some and calm for most. Both readings come from the same dataset.

Violent deaths per 100,000 · world vs. selected states2010 — 2024
Source · UCDP; ACLED — illustrative
Hope

For most of humanity, the flat world line is the lived reality: violence is now a tail risk, not a daily one.

Despair

To live inside the spike is to find the world average obscene. Sudan’s line is not a statistic to Sudan.

Confusion

Which line is “the world”? The mean, the median, or the worst-off? Each answers a different moral question.

DespairCheap drones, autonomy, and disinformation lower the cost of mass harm.

HopeThe nuclear taboo and economic entanglement still raise the cost of total war.

II The two strongest cases

Strongest case for hope

Interstate conquest has become rare and largely illegitimate. For most people alive today, the statistical chance of dying violently is the lowest it has ever been.1

Strongest case for despair

That decline reversed after 2020. Every recent signal turns upward at once,2 and the technologies that made killing expensive are becoming cheap.3

III What would change this verdict

A sustained five-year rise across all five signals would retire the long-run optimism. One nuclear exchange would end the argument entirely.

IV Methodology

Everything done to the numbers between the source and the chart, disclosed in full. Nothing here is a black box; where a choice is editorial rather than measured, we say so.

Source & vintage
Each series is pulled from the datasets listed in §V, archived at a fixed vintage. The vintage date and the dataset’s own revision are recorded in the figure’s metadata.
Transformations
Counts are converted to rates per 100,000 using mid-year population; currencies are deflated to constant 2021 USD. No series is re-based, capped, or seasonally adjusted.
Display smoothing
Lines use monotone-cubic interpolation purely for legibility. It is cosmetic — it never moves, adds, or hides a data point, and it cannot overshoot a real value.
The verdict & tally
Each signal is classed improving or worsening by the sign of its change across the visible window. The headline verdict is an editorial reading of those signals — it is deliberately not a single computed index.
The vantage toggle
“Khartoum, today” re-weights the two closest stops toward a worst-affected reading. It is illustrative of distributional reality, not a separate measured series.
