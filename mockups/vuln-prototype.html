How close to the edge? Three honest ways to measure debt fragility.

1 · Red lines crossed

Not a weighted score — a count of how many named, cited danger thresholds each country breaks. You can see exactly which. Sorted by count.

What the count hides (†): the defaulted countries — Argentina, Zimbabwe, Lebanon, Venezuela — post deceptively low interest-to-revenue because they stopped paying. Their gauges read calm precisely because they already collapsed. The count understates them; read the fingerprint, not the number.

2 · The fragility plane

Two axes refuse the lie of one. Across: can the country earn enough foreign currency to service its debt. Up: how much of the budget interest devours. Bubble size = public debt, % of GDP.

3 · The cost, in classrooms

UNCTAD's own ratio: interest payments divided by public education spending. Above 1×, a government spends more servicing creditors than teaching its children.

24 countries are past the education line; 50 past the equivalent line for health. Nigeria — which crosses only one external red line — tops this one at 6.5×. Vulnerability depends on which gauge you read.
