The chart kit

Eight charts, chosen by the job they do.

Not a catalogue of types — a small vocabulary organised by the question each chart answers. Every template bakes in the temperature colours, the baked watermark, the ↓CSV/↓SVG/↓PNG artifacts, and a plain-language summary. Pick by the job; compose; grow the set only when a real article needs something that isn't here. All data below is illustrative.

The long arcsingle line · area

Is the trend up or down over the long run?

Use when one series over a long span carries the point. The filled area gives weight; the endpoint ring marks “now.” This primitive, drawn small and repeated, becomes the lens’ signal grid.

Homicides per 100,000 / year · Western Europe1300 — 2020

The recent breakline · event annotation

Did something change lately — and when?

Use when a turning point is the story. A single dated annotation names the cause without a paragraph. Reach for it the moment a long calm line bends.

Who the average hidesmulti-series lines

Does one number mask very different realities?

Use to break a global mean apart. The flat “world” line and a country in crisis on the same axis is the whole hope-and-despair argument in one frame.

The spreadrange / dot strip

How unequal is this across places or people?

Use when distribution is the point. Each dot is a place; the dashed line is the average — and you see instantly how few sit near it. (The same primitive as the home atlas, here on real values.)

Before & afterslope chart

Who moved, and who didn’t?

Use for two moments, many entities. Slope and colour do the talking — up or down, better or worse — across a dozen lines without a legend.

The compositionstacked area · used sparingly

What is it made of, and how is the mix changing?

Use when the shifting share matters more than any one line. Kept rare and muted — stacks mislead easily, so we reach for them last, never for decoration.

The relationshipscatter · handle with care

Does one thing track another?

Use to show correlation — with a warning in the prose that it isn’t cause. One point is highlighted so a layperson has somewhere to stand before reading the cloud.

The single factstat-in-prose · not a KPI card

One number that should stop you.

Use when a figure needs no chart, only scale. Typography in the reading flow — never a boxed metric card. Always paired with a human-scale line so the number is felt, not just read.
