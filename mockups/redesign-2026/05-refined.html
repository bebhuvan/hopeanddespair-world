"Per one hundred thousand people"

Raw death counts always climb as the population grows, which makes every era look bloodier than the last. So we count deaths per 100,000 people instead — out of a crowd the size of a small city, how many die violently in a year? It lets a medieval village and a modern megacity be set side by side, honestly. The catch worth holding onto: a falling rate can hide a rising number, when the crowd grows faster than the toll.

A global average is no comfort under a drone. My street is the only dataset that matters.

The long arc

Over centuries, has everyday violence actually fallen?

Homicide has fallen for seven centuries.

Where parish and coroner records survive, the everyday violence of private life has collapsed. A medieval European was tens of times more likely to be murdered than a person living in the same cities today — a decline so long it predates the state's monopoly on force.

Hope

A forty-fold fall. The medieval baseline of violent death has all but vanished from daily life.

Despair

"Rare" is not "none," and the clean line is European — much of the world is far thinner on history.

Confusion

Definitions, coverage, and record-keeping all shift beneath the curve. The slope is real; the precision is not.

in plain words

Still lost? Read this.

The world is incredibly safe compared to the deep past, and increasingly dangerous compared to five years ago. Both of those are true at exactly the same time.

Zoom out across centuries and the chance of dying violently has collapsed — real progress, bought with laws, treaties, and the modern state. Zoom in on the last few years and the machinery of peace is buckling: more people are displaced than ever recorded, and battle deaths are climbing. The long arc bends toward peace; the recent trend has sharply broken that promise.
