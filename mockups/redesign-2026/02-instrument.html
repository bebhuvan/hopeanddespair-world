Question 01 · War & Peace

Is humanity becoming less violent?

No single chart can answer it, so we don't ask one to. Five signals are read at once — and the verdict depends entirely on how far back you set the lens. The same evidence converges, then breaks apart.

// Before you read · caveats & blind spots
  • "Violence" here means direct lethal violence — it excludes coercion, structural harm, and the threat of force.
  • Pre-1900 numbers are regional and sparse; the global long arc is reconstructed, not measured.
  • The composite verdict is an editorial reading of the signals, not a computed index.

Term, defined
"Per 100,000 people"

Raw death counts always rise as the population grows, which makes every era look bloodier than the last. So we count deaths per 100,000 people instead — out of a crowd the size of a small city, how many die violently in a year? It lets a medieval village and a modern megacity be compared honestly. The catch: a falling rate can hide a rising number.

In plain words

Still lost? Read this.

The world is incredibly safe compared to the deep past, but increasingly dangerous compared to five years ago. Zoom out across centuries and the chance of dying violently has collapsed. Zoom in on the last few years and the machinery of peace is buckling. Both of these truths exist at exactly the same time.
