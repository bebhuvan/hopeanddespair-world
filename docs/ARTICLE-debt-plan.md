# Article plan — *Is the world drowning in debt — and who actually drowns?*

The sovereign-debt deep-dive. A **new question** for the atlas (companion to Q5 wealth & poverty —
debt distress *is* how the cost-of-living crisis becomes a development reversal — and to the
forthcoming inflation/dollar piece, which spins off from the same money research). Earned by the
2020s sovereign-debt wave (Sri Lanka, Ghana, Zambia, Lebanon defaults) and UNCTAD's finding that
interest now outranks health or education spending for billions. The spine is **distribution, not
level**: "the world's debt" is at records, but it lives nowhere — and how a country goes broke,
and comes back, is the story.

## 1. The spine — the one-sentence answer

**Debt is the lever that built modern prosperity and the rope that hangs the unlucky — the same
instrument read from two ends.** World debt is at record highs, but "the world's debt rate" lives
nowhere: rich states borrow cheaply in money they print and roll it forever (Japan ~240% of GDP,
calm); poor states borrow dear in a currency they don't control, and a strong dollar can hang them
(Sri Lanka defaulted at 99%). The level is not the danger — **composition, currency, and creditor
are.** How a country goes broke is a sequence (the anchor breaks → original sin → the peg snaps →
the thermometer breaks); recovery is a known, painful cure. Hope (defaults get resolved,
stabilizations work, no collapse is fated) and despair (the burden falls hardest on those who chose
it least; billions live where interest outranks the classroom) are **the same debt read from the
two ends of who holds it.**

The thesis made visible: progress and catastrophe coexist; the answer depends on the lens — here,
on *whose balance sheet you read, and in whose currency.*

## 2. The verdict discipline (the main judgment call — confirm before build)

Recommended: **confusion, with a despair lean and the hope carried by agency.** Aggregate debt
rising is real but ambiguous (leverage, not just burden); the concentrated distress tail is the
despair; the genuine hope is that **collapse is never fated and recovery is a known cure**. Per
[[WRITING.md]] rule 8, refuse lazy both-sidesism: each movement's `take` weighs locally; the
article-level synthesis names the unresolved center — *the level isn't the risk, the distribution
is, and the same system that lets the US roll trillions hangs a small economy for a fraction of
it.* **This is the user's call** (cf. the fertility "no overall lean" decision,
[[ARTICLE-fertility-plan.md]] §2) — flagged, not assumed.

## 3. The honest-frame moves (each a finding, not a caveat)

1. **The level isn't the danger.** Japan rolls ~240% of GDP in its own currency without crisis;
   Sri Lanka defaulted at ~99%. Debt-to-GDP alone predicts almost nothing — the reframe the whole
   piece is built to deliver (M3).
2. **The gauges lie where it's worst.** Defaulted countries (Argentina, Lebanon, Zimbabwe,
   Venezuela) post deceptively *low* interest-to-revenue **because they stopped paying** — the
   composite reads calm precisely where collapse already happened. Any vulnerability score that
   ranks them "healthy" is wrong; the disclosure *is* a finding (M8).
3. **Data availability inversely tracks crisis.** WB government-debt %GDP comes back empty for the
   crisis cast; fiscal series freeze (Egypt at 2015, Venezuela after 2016). The worse the collapse,
   the sparser the official number — the M6 "can we trust the number" theme generalized from CPI to
   the whole fiscal surface. A coverage-decay chart in its own right (M8).
4. **Who you owe is who decides your fate.** Lebanon owes **94%** to private creditors (hard,
   dollar-market, near-unrestructurable); Pakistan/Zimbabwe **~9%** (official/bilateral). Same
   distress, opposite exposure — "original sin" made measurable (M6).

## 5. The named tensions (the insights the charts exist to deliver)

1. **The level isn't the danger** — composition, currency, and creditor are (Japan 240% vs Sri Lanka 99%).
2. **The world's debt lives nowhere** — records in aggregate, concentrated distress in a tail.
3. **Who you owe is who decides your fate** — private/market debt hangs harder than official (Lebanon 94%).
4. **The gauges lie where it's worst** — defaulted countries read calm; data thins as crisis deepens.
5. **Debt is measured in classrooms** — 24 countries spend more on interest than education, 50 than health.
6. **Original sin** — borrowing in money you don't print turns a US rate hike into your crisis.
7. **Collapse is a choice; recovery is a known cure** — no country is fated, but the cure is painful
   and unevenly available.

## 6. Data — integration status & the license split (the make-or-break)

> **The user's directive:** *re-derive every data point from source* — UNCTAD, BIS, IMF, WB, ILO —
> not from the publication Excel. Drives the `unctad` adapter below (pull from the **Data Hub**,
> unctadstat, NOT the World of Debt report spreadsheet — see the licence note).

**The re-hostable spine (CC BY / CC0 / PD / CC BY 3.0 IGO):**
- **UNCTAD Data Hub** (CC BY 3.0 IGO — re-host with attribution; [[unctad-license-rehost]]). Public
  debt (%GDP, US$, per-capita), external public debt, **creditor composition** (bilateral /
  multilateral / private), interest payments (%GDP / %revenue / per-capita), **interest-vs-education**
  and **interest-vs-health** ratios, government / education / health expenditure. 200 countries,
  2010–2024. *NEW `unctad` adapter — re-derive from unctadstat, the make-or-break re-host.*
- **World Bank IDS + WDI** (CC BY 4.0, existing `worldbank` adapter): short-term debt %reserves
  (`DT.DOD.DSTC.IR.ZS`), reserves in months of imports (`FI.RES.TOTL.MO`), debt service %exports
  (`DT.TDS.DECT.EX.ZS`), external debt %GNI (`DT.DOD.DECT.GN.ZS`), net migration (`SM.POP.NETM`),
  remittances %GDP (`BX.TRF.PWKR.DT.GD.ZS`), resource rents, fuel exports. **All confirmed live to 2024.**
- **Jordà-Schularick-Taylor Macrohistory DB** (open): 1870– advanced-economy debt/credit/crises —
  the long view (M1) and recovery base-rates (M12). Small new static ingest.
- **OWID** (CC BY, existing adapter): any long-run debt/context series.
- **BoC–BoE Sovereign Default Database** (1960–2024, Bank of Canada Valet API, group `DEBT_2025`):
  the modern **default census** — debt *in default* by creditor (IMF/IBRD/IDA/IADB/Paris Club/**China**/
  other-official/private), by instrument (FC bonds, FC bank loans, LC debt, arrears), by group
  (advanced/EME/HIPC/SSA), plus **default rates** vs world public debt and world GDP. Powers M6
  (the China-creditor shift), M11 (the collapse cases), and M12 (the hope read: default stock *fell*
  to US$425bn in 2024, just **0.4%** of global public debt; **13 sovereigns = 90%** of it — the
  distress-tail thesis, now for defaults). **Licence is a deliberate call:** BoC's custom terms
  explicitly grant "freely use, copy, distribute and transmit … with attribution" (CC BY-equivalent)
  but are not on the CC-only re-host whitelist — either widen the gate with a documented exception
  (recommended) or treat link-only. *NEW small `boc` adapter (Valet API JSON/CSV).*

**The link-only layer (chart, cite, link out; never re-host the CSV):**
- **BIS** ([[bis-licence-link-only]], existing `bis` adapter — confirmed keys): total credit/debt

## 7. New adapters & the derived composites (the genuinely new build)

1. **`unctad`** (NEW · re-host · CC BY 3.0 IGO) — the make-or-break. Pull from the **Data Hub
   (unctadstat)** SDMX/bulk-CSV, not the WoD publication spreadsheet. Sets the CC BY 3.0 IGO licence
   string; rides the existing re-host gate. Powers M2, M3, M6, M7, and feeds the composites.
2. **`worldbank`** (exists) — register the IDS indicators above.
3. **`bis`** (exists) — `WS_TC` / `WS_DSR` / `WS_GLI`, link-only.
4. **`imf`** (NEW · link-only) *or* cite-only — HPDD / GDD. Build only if M1/M9 need the deep fiscal
   series and openness checks out; otherwise cite.
5. **`ilostat`** (exists) — real wages, if Act III is deepened.
6. **JST** — small static ingest (open), deep history.
7. **`boc`** (NEW · re-host-with-attribution *or* link-only — see §6) — Bank of Canada Valet API,
   group `DEBT_2025`. JSON/CSV; one fetch returns the whole BoC–BoE default census. Powers M6/M11/M12.

**Derived composites — the signature, and the methodology that keeps them honest:**
- `debt.redlines_crossed` — **count** of named, cited thresholds a country breaks (NOT a weighted
  score). The six lines, each with a source: ST-debt > 100% reserves (Guidotti-Greenspan); reserves
  < 3 months; debt service > 25% of exports; debt > 70% GDP; interest > 20% of revenue; interest >
  education. Recipe records every line + threshold + source.
- `debt.fragility_plane` — the `(x = debt service %exports, y = interest %revenue, size = debt %GDP,
  flag = defaulted)` tuple per country. A 2D position, never collapsed to 1D.
- *The cost-in-classrooms ratio comes straight from UNCTAD — not derived; no invented number.*

> **Composite methodology box (must be airtight — this is novel for the atlas):**
> - **No black-box weighted index.** A vulnerability score with arbitrary weights is exactly the
>   false precision the atlas distrusts. Use threshold-counts, cited ratios, and 2D position.
> - **The defaulted-distortion flag is a feature.** Countries in default/restructuring post
>   artificially low interest ratios; every composite flags them "low because it stopped paying, not
>   because it's healthy." (Named tension #4 / M8.)
> - **Show the components — always.** The fingerprint *is* the disclosure; the count shows *which*
>   lines crossed.
> - **Every composite is a derived series with a recorded recipe** ([[DATA.md]] §4); the manual
>   number audit applies as to any other series.

## 8. The recurring device

**The fragility fingerprint runs through.** Each cautionary tale (M9–M11) replays the same gauge-set
as a small-multiple over time, so the reader *watches* the sequence of collapse — the anchor breaks,
reserves drain, debt-service spikes, default, the gauges go quiet (the thermometer dying, M8). The
number to watch (what-would-change-it): not global debt/GDP but **the size of the distress tail** —
how many countries cross ≥4 red lines, and whether interest-vs-education keeps climbing. The verdict
is carried by the charts' contradiction (M1's record aggregate against M3's "level isn't danger" and
M12's real recoveries), not resolved in prose.
