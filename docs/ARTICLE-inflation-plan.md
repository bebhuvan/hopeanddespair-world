# Article plan — *Is inflation under control — and whose currency decides?*

The money / inflation / dollar deep-dive. A **new question** for the atlas (not in the original
twelve — see [[DATASET-ATLAS.md]] backlog), earned by the 2021–23 surge: the worst global
inflation in ~40 years, now receding in the rich world but not at the periphery. The reason the
burden split that way is the spine of the piece, and it is **the dollar**. Companion to Q5 (wealth
& poverty — the cost-of-living shock is felt as poverty) and Q3/Q4 (the energy-price trigger).

## 1. The spine — the one-sentence answer

**Inflation looks like a story about prices; it is really a story about one currency.** The
2021–23 fever — the worst in two generations — has broken in the advanced world and *not* at the
periphery, and the reason the burden split that way runs through the dollar: the world borrows,
trades, and saves in money whose price the US Federal Reserve sets. When a US-centred shock forces
the Fed to hike, the dollar strengthens and the cost lands hardest on countries that borrowed in
dollars and import in dollars. Hope (the fever broke; inflation targeting works; hyperinflations
are rarer than the 1990s) and despair (the poor world pays for the rich world's cure) are **the
same fact read from the two ends of the dollar.**

The thesis made visible: progress and catastrophe coexist; the answer depends on the lens — here,
on *which currency you hold*.

## 2. The verdict discipline (the main judgment call — confirm before build)

Recommended: **confusion, with a despair spine.** The global average getting "back under control"
is real (hope), but it is the lens that hides the asymmetry the whole piece is built to expose.
Per [[WRITING.md]] rule 8, refuse lazy both-sidesism: each movement's `take` still weighs locally
(says which read is heavier *for that chart*); the article-level synthesis names the asymmetry as
the unresolved center — the fever broke unevenly *by design of the monetary system*, and that is
neither a clean win nor a clean loss. **This is the user's call** (cf. the fertility "no overall
lean" decision, [[ARTICLE-fertility-plan.md]] §2) — flagged, not assumed.

## 3. The honest-frame moves (each a finding, not a caveat)

1. **"The world's inflation rate" lives nowhere.** A global CPI line is an average of advanced
   economies near 2% and a crisis tail (Argentina, Türkiye, Egypt, Sudan, Zimbabwe, Venezuela)
   in the double-to-quadruple digits. The average is the half-truth; the spread is the story (M4–M6).
2. **Can we even trust the number?** Argentina's official CPI was politically suppressed
   (INDEC, ~2007–2015); Venezuela stopped publishing. The data-quality gap *is* part of the answer
   — epistemic-status tags do heavy lifting here (M6), exactly as [[DATASET-ATLAS.md]] Q6 prescribes.
3. **Nobody voted for the Fed.** The country whose central bank effectively sets the world's
   interest rate is the one the rest of the world cannot vote in (Act III). "Exorbitant privilege"
   stated plainly, then shown.

## 5. The named tensions (the insights the charts exist to deliver)

1. **The fever broke unevenly — by design.** The same dollar system that anchors rich-world prices
   transmits the shock to the periphery. (the core asymmetry / spine)
2. **The world average lives nowhere** — near-2% advanced economies and a triple-digit crisis tail
   share one meaningless mean.
3. **The thermometer can be broken** — where inflation is worst, the official number is least
   trustworthy (Argentina, Venezuela). Measurement is political.
4. **One country's central bank is the world's** — the Fed fights US inflation; the dollar's rise
   bills the rest of the world in debt service and import costs.
5. **A strong dollar is a tax on the indebted** — dollar up ⇒ dollar-debt repayment + dollar-priced
   imports up, for borrowers who never chose the rate.
6. **Targeting won, hyperinflation lost — mostly** — the long view is genuinely hopeful (fewer
   hyperinflations than the 1980s–90s); the tail refuses to disappear.
7. **"Under control" is a rich-world sentence** — true where you can read it in English; not where
   the cost-of-living crisis is still the headline.

## 6. Data — integration status & the license split (the make-or-break)

**The verifiable spine (re-hostable, CC BY / PD — mostly existing adapters):**
- World Bank **`FP.CPI.TOTL.ZG`** (inflation, consumer prices, annual %, 1960–) + **`FP.CPI.TOTL`**
  (index) + GDP-deflator **`NY.GDP.DEFL.KD.ZG`** as a cross-check — existing `worldbank` adapter,
  zero new code. **Verify at ingest:** WB CPI is sourced from IMF IFS, so check the per-indicator
  metadata for the third-party-redistribution flag; if flagged, fall back to **OWID own-processed**
  inflation series (`owid` adapter) which is clean CC BY. (Known gate trap, [[DATA.md]] §9.)
- World Bank **income-group & regional aggregates** — the open substitute for IMF WEO's
  advanced-vs-emerging split (IMF is link-only; cite it, chart the WB cut).
- **FAO Food Price Index** (CC BY) + WB/OWID energy prices — the M3 trigger overlay.
- **Federal Reserve H.10 — Nominal Broad U.S. Dollar Index** (US-gov **PD** → re-host). Source
  **direct from federalreserve.gov**, *not* via FRED (FRED's terms forbid bulk re-hosting). The DXY
  itself is ICE-proprietary → link-only; the Fed broad index is the open analog. *(small new `fed`
  adapter — the one re-hostable new build.)*

**The dollar spine (link-only — chart, cite, link out for download; never re-host the CSV):**
- **BIS Global Liquidity Indicators** — *US-dollar credit to non-bank borrowers outside the US*
  (M9, the exorbitant-privilege chart) — genuinely BIS-unique, **no open mirror exists anywhere**.
- **BIS central-bank policy rates** (M7, the synchronized hiking cycle).
- **BIS debt-service ratios** + **credit to the non-financial sector** (M10, the EM debt burden).
- (Optional) BIS effective exchange rates (REER/NEER) — Fed broad index covers the dollar leg, so
  BIS EER is reserve, not required.

> **Licensing verdict (researched 2026-06-16):** BIS statistics are **not** CC BY/CC0/PD — a custom
> BIS-copyright licence whose *statistics terms* permit display/charting with attribution but fail
> the re-host whitelist. So: **display freely, download links out to `data.bis.org`.** Exactly the
> existing **link-only** pattern (EM-DAT in the climate M5, [[DATASET-ATLAS.md]] Q3) — the `bis`
> adapter sets the BIS-terms licence string and the gate suppresses the `↓ data` artifact, emitting
> a `↓ source` link instead. IMF and FRED are likewise link-only for re-hosting.

**New adapters required (resist building speculatively, [[DATA.md]] §1.7 — build at article time):**
1. **`bis`** (link-only) — the one genuinely new build. Prefer the **bulk CSV downloads** over the
   SDMX REST API per-row calls (snapshot-friendly, fewer rate limits, [[DATA.md]] §3). Powers M7,
   M9, M10. Reuses the existing link-only gate machinery — no new gate plumbing.
2. **`fed`** (PD, re-host) — small; Federal Reserve H.10 broad-dollar index. Powers M8.
3. CPI / aggregates / FAO / energy ride the **existing `worldbank` + `owid`** adapters.

**Gap register (named in the piece, not charted):**
- **IMF WEO** advanced-vs-emerging inflation aggregates & projections — the canonical split, but
  link-only → cite, chart the open WB income-group cut instead.
- **Real wages** (prices vs pay — the lived cost-of-living crisis): WB/ILO real-wage series — verify
  CC BY; one ingest away if Act I is deepened. The current plan shows the price level, not the squeeze.
- **Venezuela / Argentina "true" inflation estimates** (Hanke / academic reconstructions) —
  link-only, cited in M6 with an epistemic tag.
- **DXY** (ICE) — link-only; the Fed broad index is the re-hostable stand-in (M8).

## 7. The recurring device

**The dollar runs through, it is not parked in Act III.** The Fed policy rate (M7) and the broad
dollar index (M8) return as a faint overlaid line on the burden charts (M4, M5, M9, M10), so the
reader *sees* the periphery's inflation and debt-service pain rise as the dollar rises — the spine
argument made visual, repeated until it lands. The number to watch (what-would-change-it): not the
global CPI average but **the advanced-vs-periphery inflation gap**, and whether the world's
dollar-denominated debt keeps growing faster than the economies that owe it. The verdict is carried
by the charts' contradiction (M2's hope against M5/M9's despair), not resolved in prose.
